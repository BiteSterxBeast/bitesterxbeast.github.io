Video Downloader

A short, honest list of trustworthy ways to save YouTube videos for offline viewing — pointing you to the right tool instead of building a risky one ourselves.

Why this is a list and not a built-in tool: Actually extracting YouTube video streams requires bypassing signed, constantly-rotating protections YouTube puts in place specifically to stop this — which means running a maintained backend server, not something a static site like this one can safely or reliably do. Public "YouTube downloader" sites have also historically drawn takedown notices and legal action. Rather than build something that breaks in a few weeks and creates legal risk, this page points to tools that already do it properly.

01 YouTube Premium

Official

The only fully sanctioned way to download YouTube videos — built directly into the YouTube app for offline playback. No third-party software, no gray areas, no risk of your download breaking after a YouTube update.

💰 Subscription required 📱 Mobile / TV app only
Get YouTube Premium ↗

02 cobalt

Open Source No Ads

A free, open-source web app for saving video, audio, photos, and GIFs — not just from YouTube, but most of your favorite sites. No ads, trackers, or paywalls, with a strict zero-log policy and processing that happens directly on your device whenever possible for maximum privacy. Fully self-hostable if you'd rather run your own instance.

🆓 Free 🌐 Web-based — just paste a link 🔒 Zero-log, privacy-first
Visit cobalt ↗

03 yt-dlp

Open Source

The tool technical users trust most, precisely because its code is fully public and auditable on GitHub — there's nothing hidden to worry about. Actively maintained by a large open-source community that keeps up with YouTube's changes. Command-line based, so it takes a bit more comfort with a terminal than a one-click app.

🆓 Free 💻 Command line 🖥️ Windows / Mac / Linux

Staying safe with any downloader

  • Only download software from a tool's official website — search results and ad banners for "YouTube downloader" are full of lookalike scam sites.
  • Be suspicious of any "downloader" asking you to install a browser extension you've never heard of, or bundling extra software during install.
  • Run a quick antivirus scan on anything you download before opening it, especially free tools.
  • If a site is covered in pop-ups, fake "Download" buttons, or redirects before you even reach the actual tool — close it and move on.
⚠️ Downloading a video doesn't change who owns it. These tools are meant for personal offline viewing, backups, or content you have the rights to — not for reuploading or redistributing someone else's work.
